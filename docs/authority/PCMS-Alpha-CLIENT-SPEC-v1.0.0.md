## 0. How to use and update this file

1. **Review one cohesive product process per discussion, with multiple related questions per turn.** Think through options and consequences before asking. Clearly mark **RECOMMENDED** defaults, alternatives and client-only choices. Capture expected actions/results in ordinary language, including real examples and exceptional cases. Do not start designing new state machines or CI gates before agreeing on the behavior.
2. Ask a useful bundle of related decisions in each conversation turn (typically 4–6), not one question per turn. State the root problem, options, recommendation and concrete trade-off. **Do not record the recommended answer as ACCEPTED until the client chooses it.** Keep accepted decisions in **§1** and their detailed contracts in **§2**. Update question status in **§3** and checklist in **§4** **after every explicit agreement**.
3. Mark every substantial item as one of: **ACCEPTED** (client explicitly chose it), **PROPOSED** (engineering recommendation awaiting decision), **OPEN**, or **IMPLEMENTED / LIVE VERIFIED** (requires actual relevant evidence). An accepted behavior is **not** automatically implemented or verified.
4. Record a short dated **decision log** in **§6** whenever a decision is added, modified, or superseded. Never rewrite history silently; preserve the superseded position and why it changed.
5. This is the **accepted client product-behavior contract** as of v1.0.0. It is not yet an approved replacement of the signed Stage 7 **engineering/security baseline**. Engineering feasibility, actual conflicts and any technical-authority transition require a separate review and explicit approval; signed originals remain intact.
6. Future behavior changes require an **explicit client decision, versioned amendment, dated change log and corresponding acceptance-test update**. Never silently alter the accepted product. The local file is not automatically synced to GitHub or earlier downloads; attach the latest approved version for future sessions.

## 2. Plain-language operator contracts and representative acceptance journeys

### 2.1 Account and Persona lifecycle

**What the client does:** Clicks **Add Account**; PCMS opens the correct Perchance login tab; client signs in once and clicks **Confirm**. In unusual Cloudflare situations, the client can choose **Change route** directly in PCMS. Routine PersonaMonkey UI interaction is not required.

**What PCMS does:** Allocate a new or existing protected Mullvad route with a **unique verified exit**, provision it through PersonaMonkey if needed, create and bind a dedicated Persona, open tabs in its container, independently detect the authenticated account, synchronize generator inventory, and remember the binding. For subsequent jobs PCMS opens and manages its own tabs in the correct Persona automatically.

**During failure:** Recover a failed route automatically when safe; if necessary provision a different distinct protected exit, then revalidate the Perchance session. Pause only affected accounts/jobs. Never switch to Direct, reuse another account's recorded exit IP, or conflate an HTTP/Cloudflare challenge with route failure. If the user is required to log in again, retain queue and history. A different detected login must not silently replace the bound identity.

**Strict exit-history policy:** Track observed account/IP assignments, persist through archive and backup, avoid known cross-account historical reuse. Distinguish verified observations from unknown prior history; PCMS cannot guarantee uniqueness across unobserved external Mullvad users or before it began recording exits. Treat IPv4 and IPv6 identity paths conservatively.

**Account archival, reactivation and local removal (C-56–C-60 — ACCEPTED):**
- **Archive:** one operator action suspends this account indefinitely but reversibly. Keep its Persona/session where usable, identity, generators, source, history and restore context. Do not change remote visibility, ownership or source because of archival. Archived accounts receive no new automatic allocations; this is distinct from temporary unavailability.
- **Safe checkpoint:** stop initiating provider writes; safely close or pause jobs at a stable boundary, observing any ambiguous already-dispatched effect before taking further action. Long jobs need not run to completion. Reassign only never-attempted automatic batch allocations as already specified in C-35; retain explicit/potentially attempted associations. Other accounts continue.
- **Idle route:** release unnecessary live route resources without discarding the Persona or letting another account automatically use its historically observed exits. On return, restore or replace with a freshly verified, permissible protected route; never use Direct fallback.
- **Reactivate:** automatically verify original identity, account session, distinct protected exit and latest owned inventory, reconcile pending operations, and resume only still-eligible work. Request login when the session expired or verification needs human action. A new replacement account never silently assumes the prior account's generators.
- **Remove local account data:** offer a separately confirmed action to delete selected local credentials/session and obsolete account data. Present a precise deletion/retention summary. Do not delete remote generators, original source/backups or indispensable historical exit-IP reservations. Prospective backup/removal retention follows accepted C-72–C-75; do not silently change historical copies.

**Archival acceptance example:** Account A has 100 owned generators, 25 in Refresher active periods, and a deployment in progress. Archive A: PCMS prevents new A writes, checkpoints/reconciles A's current work, suspends A's refreshing, releases idle route resources if safe and keeps its Persona/source/session/exit history. Other accounts continue. Reactivate later: PCMS verifies A's original account, session, ownership and permissible route, then resumes only eligible work without asking for a new account binding. Its active/rest clocks follow the previously accepted healthy-time/pause rules.

**Acceptance examples:**
- First account: only PCMS Add Account, login, Confirm; no PersonaMonkey manual preparation.
- Second account: if a route resolves to the first account's exit, PCMS provisions/selects a different one before enabling jobs.
- No unused verified exit: affected enrollment is held and explained; other accounts remain usable.
- Route loses connectivity: PCMS repairs/replaces, reconfirms isolation and identity, resumes only eligible jobs.
- Cloudflare challenge: PCMS does **not** automatically churn routes; offers human challenge and Change route actions.
- Cold Firefox restart: PCMS recovers queue/bindings without requiring repeated confirmations when the session remains valid.

### 2.2 Generator creation, adoption and batch allocation

**What the client does:** May submit one slug or many inside PCMS, optionally selecting specific accounts; otherwise allocation uses **Automatic — balanced** across managed non-archived accounts. Alternatively, the client or a separate authoring agent adds a recognized `generators/<slug>/` folder containing `DEPLOYMENT.md` on a separate Git branch, and the operator or independent verifier merges that change into GitHub **`main`**: PCMS detects the merged entry and initiates the same generator-creation/reconciliation workflow without a second Start action. Unmerged branches/PRs are not execution inputs. Neither trigger requires the operator to manually prepare PersonaMonkey or individually approve technical substeps. New Perchance **accounts** are enrolled only on an explicit operator-initiated Add Account action (C-79).

**What PCMS does:** From a PCMS slug request **or a recognized GitHub generator entry**, durably coalesce identical creation intents, synchronize confirmed owned counts plus pending assignments, distribute new work toward balanced totals, prepare the assigned Persona/tab, create real empty private/unlisted generators, rename and verify target/owner, and initialize or associate the Git folder without overwriting source. A Git-originated creation must not turn an incomplete/non-READY folder into a deployment: only a coherent READY declaration starts source deployment. Existing verified-owned generators are adopted under standing account authorization **without changing their live source or visibility merely because they were adopted**. Under contention, reconcile uncertain in-flight effects before new discretionary jobs and adapt concurrency within configured maximums (C-77/C-78).

**Existing-generator adoption and source initialization — ACCEPTED:**
- **Missing GitHub folder:** first capture an independently restorable local snapshot of the exact existing Perchance `main.pjs`, `index.html` and generator-owned `src/` source. Initialize the correctly associated GitHub folder by committing that **same original source** plus non-READY `DEPLOYMENT.md`. Record the original-source commit and fingerprints. This is a baseline, **not** a deployment or publication event.
- **Existing unbound GitHub folder:** check the actual verified Perchance owner/slug and existing PCMS source-folder associations. - **Existing READY candidate differs from published source:** preserve the original/previous-good live source and queue the READY revision for automatic development/testing. For an existing public generator, do not unlist or stage until its eligible update/sleep window. Offer **Update now** only as an explicit, safe override. **Approval to publish the final candidate remains human-owned.**

- **Archived account (reversible Archive under C-56):** redistribute only never-attempted, automatically allocated items; explicit account pins and attempted/uncertain items retain their association pending resolution.
- **Temporarily unavailable account:** retain it in new-batch fair allocation while it remains managed; hold its own jobs, not healthy accounts' jobs.

- **Concurrent/repeated batches:** merge compatible duplicate slug requests into one durable operation, retaining each batch's own view; only conflicting explicit target choices require an operator decision.
- **Partial failures/restarts:** resume independently safe item steps, reconcile unknown external effects before any retry, and report actionable items without interrupting unrelated work.

**Failure and concurrency rules:**
- One account: use that account; **no invented per-account per-batch cap**.
- Temporary outage: preserve allocated work for that account, continue independent healthy work.
- Duplicate slugs within/between batches: deduplicate or merge work without duplicate remote creation.
- Requested slug already owned by a managed account: reconcile/adopt under **the actual verified owner**; do not recreate.
- Slug affirmatively owned externally: mark conflict; never invent replacement slugs.
- Missing/unreliable owner observation: hold only affected slug; absence is not proof of ownership/deletion.
- Creation may have happened but ACK was lost: read-only reconcile; **no blind retry or reassignment**.
- GitHub unavailable: preserve confirmed remote creation; queue only Git work.
- Existing Git folder bound to another generator, or ambiguous pre-existing content: do not overwrite; actionable exception.
- **Browser restart or parallel batches:** use one durable operation per target slug across compatible batch requests; resume only known-safe steps after restart and reconcile uncertain effects without reissuing writes. - Very large batches: use bounded concurrency/backpressure and progress/resume; **no arbitrary product-level count cap**.

**Divergent pre-existing source (C-34):** The original live source must be captured in a separately pinned Git reference plus independent local backup, with zero overwrite of the active development tree. Whether one shared archival ref, a lightweight object/ref scheme or another minimal reversible design is appropriate must be assessed against Git scalability and managed-path restrictions; this is a **technical implementation choice**, not an unresolved product decision.

### 2.3 Unified generator deployment and update

**One conceptual job:** `Deploy(generator, candidate)` with `INITIAL` or `UPDATE` context, **not two divergent AI/testing/publishing pipelines**.

**Entry:** Relevant GitHub `Status: READY` and valid managed source; actual account, Persona, route, ownership and source association available. New private empty generator may stage immediately. Existing published generator (including newly adopted public source) **remains published** until its allowed update/sleep period, preserves the prior-good source, and is unlisted before source modification under the existing update policy. **Update now** is an operator-initiated exception that must still preserve prior-good data and human unsaved edits.

**Listing-state clarification (FR-03, editorial):** Before an existing public update is **admitted**, keep the generator's current public listing untouched. Once the update is admitted and unlisted, preserve the last published **source and recovery record**; do **not** claim the generator stays public during AI repair or human publication review. Keep it unlisted pending correct reviewed publication or a separately authorized recovery action. Apply the accepted cap on concurrent public-update holds (C-87). A queued READY revision by itself does not unlist the generator. This paragraph does not approve silent re-publication.


**Autonomous work:** Open/manage automation-owned editor tab; fetch/pin `main.pjs`, `index.html`, all generator-owned `src/` bytes; stage and independently observe saves; start Perchance native AI helper with GitHub `default-chat1.md`; autonomously test/debug/fix/retest; if `TESTING.md` exists run optional **fresh** Chat 2 as established in Stage 3; converge tests on the **same final full-source revision**; perform permitted conditional, nonforced GitHub writeback (not by granting AI Git credentials); queue candidate for human publication review.

**Authoritative source boundary:** AI may modify the selected generator's PJS, HTML and `src/` files under the accepted Stage 3 F2 amendment, but must not control PCMS management, repository credentials, publication or unrelated generators. Only managed paths are read/written by PCMS. Changing only unrelated GitHub files must not invalidate the deployment.

**Background operation:** The UI is not required to remain open. Jobs survive event-page suspend, browser restart and bounded provider outages. Multiple generators may progress concurrently according to route/ownership limits, but one generator never has conflicting source-changing actors.

**Failure:** Retain last known-good source and human edits. Preserve a resumable job when state is known; hold and investigate if remote effect ambiguous; do not automatically publish stale or known-failing source. The operator should generally see only `Working`, `Waiting`, `Needs attention` or `Ready for review` with details available on demand.

**Accepted READY and Git workflow details (D1–D6):** A new candidate is declared in a coherent `DEPLOYMENT.md` update with managed source (C-40), rather than inferred from intermediate commits. Before the eligible update window, retain only the newest declared revision (C-41). If a newer READY declaration arrives during AI work, checkpoint/supersede the old candidate without overwriting either source, then proceed to the latest eligible candidate (C-42). Successfully tested AI changes are conditionally written back to permitted Git paths automatically under standing policy (C-43). Concurrent relevant source edits are preserved as conflicts for reconciliation, not force-pushed or auto-merged (C-44). Prompt/`TESTING.md` inputs are pinned at job start; prompt-only changes affect future jobs rather than restarting the fleet (C-45).

**Important implementation distinction:** A PCMS-owned Git writeback must **not** accidentally masquerade as a fresh external READY candidate and restart the same deployment in a loop. External READY declarations, in-progress AI work, and final PCMS writeback require distinct causality/provenance and idempotent observations. The minimal mechanism is an engineering question; this is not a new operator confirmation step.

**Accepted AI-repair/testing controls (E1–E4):** PCMS continues autonomous test/fix cycles; the **operator**, not a speculative algorithm, decides whether further AI repair has become unproductive. The operator can Pause / Resume / Give new instructions without controlling each iteration. Checkpoints and concrete error/resource limits remain automatic. Genuine failed required tests block Ready; actual helper/test infrastructure unavailability permits transparently labeled individual manual testing; incomplete optional Chat 2 permits individually warned exceptional review after recovery attempts, never a fictitious PASS (C-46–C-49).

### 2.4 Publication and review

**What the client does:** Opens the PCMS Publication queue, inspects each **ready** candidate and clicks **Approve** (or rejects/requests further AI work). Multiple preview tabs can remain open at once. Approval belongs to the PCMS candidate record, **not** to tab activity/focus.

**What PCMS does:** Shows pending/developing/blocked/ready candidates, final source diff and test results; pins each approval to exact generator/account/final source; checks that no new AI edit, Git relevant change or human source change has invalidated the candidate; publishes the approved source and verifies resulting public state. A change after review produces a new candidate, not silent reuse of approval.

**Non-goals:** No per-tab approval ledger or requirement to keep a particular UI/editor tab open. Maintain only the internal tracking needed not to overwrite a human's dirty editor or race automation writes.

**Approval is genuinely consequential; no per-step publication ceremony.** One operator approval for the complete tested candidate; PCMS performs subsequent technical steps automatically. **Request changes** returns that exact candidate to autonomous AI development and independent retesting; a changed candidate needs new review. A newer coherent READY candidate supersedes the old unapproved review candidate. Clean unchanged fully-tested candidates may be selected for bulk approval, whereas modified/exceptional ones require individual approval. Human editing is preserved and exposed as **Adopt as new candidate / Defer**, never silently overwritten (C-50–C-53).

### 2.5 Refresher

**Primary client objective (C-54):** Occupy as much of Perchance’s actual public recent-generators page with owned eligible generators, for as much time as sustainably possible. Success is measured against **observed public-page occupancy over time** and actual provider outcomes, not simply how many generators are ACTIVE, how many saves were acknowledged, or whether a scheduler cycle completed. This is a goal, not a promise to defeat Perchance ranking/filtering or guarantee that any save appears on the page. The distinction between *fair rotation* and *maximum observable occupancy* must govern the engineering reconciliation.

**What the client does:** Enables Refresher once; optionally adjusts active/sleep durations, capacity/Pauses and selection. Routine operation thereafter is automatic.

**What PCMS does:** Maintain one global scheduler over an intentionally large inventory (1,000+ generators, across multiple separately bound accounts). During each generator’s eligible active period, observe its exact slug presence/rank through the structured recent-list endpoint (historical target cadence: about every 10 minutes). The preferred routine trigger for another controlled marker save is **confirmed disappearance from the currently observed recent page while the generator is still active**; avoid unnecessary saves while it remains present. A warranted save modifies only an approved PCMS-owned nonfunctional source marker via its authenticated Perchance editor and separately verifies the persisted bytes and recent-list outcome. Preserve bounded rates, active/sleep clocks, Pauses, account isolation and recovery. This states a *desired operator behavior*, not a claim that current I17 implements it or that it is known to be effective on the live provider.

**Why 1,000+ generators matter (C-55):** The fleet is intentionally larger than the approximately 283 slots the client has observed on the recent page (an estimate, not a guaranteed fixed size). Generators can be on the page or eligible to be refreshed while active, then relinquish routine maintenance for at least 72 hours while others become active. Individual operational timers and the global oldest-eligible-first scheduler remain the historical model; this does **not** require synchronized cohorts or a separate campaign scheduler. Resting generators ordinarily remain public; resting is not deletion or unlisting.

**Representative operator example:** Generator A is active and visible: PCMS observes it but does not save merely to reset a timer. Later, a complete recent-page observation no longer contains A: PCMS may make the single authorized source-marker save and verify whether it reappears. If its 24 hours of qualified active time expire, A enters at least 72 hours of qualified rest and is no longer routinely refreshed; the scheduler considers another eligible generator. If the write or visibility outcome is unknown/ineffective, PCMS diagnoses and holds/reconciles as appropriate rather than assuming success or blindly churning saves.

**Empirical motivation, not guaranteed provider behavior:** The client reports seeing generator-level bans only after roughly 2–3+ days of continuous refreshing. The roughly 24h/72h pattern was chosen in light of that experience, along with the need for turnover and rest; the reported threshold has not been independently established as a general Perchance rule. A complete-feed absence means **absent from that snapshot**, not necessarily banned/private/deleted. A save does not guarantee admission; never treat repetitive ineffective writes or source-marker variations as a provider-filter bypass.

**Existing scheduling intent:** Default approximately 24 hours of substantiated healthy operational active time / at least 72 hours of similarly accounted rest, oldest-eligible-first, freeze qualified clocks during downtime/holds, ~10-minute monitoring, global/account/generator Pauses, and release-work interaction. These established requirements are not rescinded by the new occupancy goal. Precise ranking-aware save selection, empirically sustainable capacity and any different trigger beyond observed disappearance remain **OPEN for engineering review**, not silently approved.

## 5. Engineering charter supplied by the client — 16 principles

The following principles govern technical reviews and implementations **after** product intent is established. They do not permit the engineer to invent new client requirements or substitute a safety ceremony for usable outcomes.

1. **First-Principles Reasoning:** Deconstruct problems to fundamental truths, invariants and real compute constraints. Avoid cargo cults.
2. **Systems Thinking:** Map lifecycles, dependencies, coupling, failure domains and compute/memory/I/O/network limits.
3. **Exhaustive Edge Cases & Future-Proofing:** Identify boundaries, races, partial failures, corruption, schema changes and scale effects.
4. **Engineering Rigor & Best Practices:** Require maintainable types/interfaces, idempotency where applicable, testability, observability and genuinely working production code.
5. **Radical Candor & Diligence:** Challenge incorrect assumptions immediately, with evidence and real trade-offs.
6. **Total Ownership & Blind-Spot Elimination:** Own the outcome end to end; identify consequential omitted hazards and Day-2 burdens, **without speculative feature expansion**.
7. **Pragmatic Parsimony & Anti-Complexity:** Every abstraction, dependency and line of code must justify its operational cost; prefer the simplest sufficient design.
8. **Forensic Observability by Construction:** Capture redacted, causal, structured diagnostic information at critical state/effect boundaries; do not make debugging depend on attached devtools.
9. **High-Density Communication & Zero Sycophancy:** Communicate decisions, failures, evidence and next actions with high signal and little filler.
10. **Reversible Decision Architecture:** Explicitly classify one-way versus reversible choices; favor rollback-friendly interfaces and explain compromises.
11. **Cognitive Ergonomics & Operational Sanity:** Favor clear flows and deterministic debugging for an engineer under incident pressure.
12. **Evolution & Transition Mechanics:** Plan incremental rollout, compatibility, state transitions and rollback; reject fragile big-bang replacement.
13. **Problem Reframing & Root-Cause Interrogation:** Identify XY problems and upstream simplifications before layering controls on symptoms.
14. **Static Stability & Blast-Radius Partitioning:** Keep unrelated accounts/generators operational under partial failure; apply bounded queues, backpressure and scoped holds.
15. **Subtractive Interface Craft & Ergonomics:** Compact, keyboard-efficient, platform-consistent interaction with unambiguous pending/failed/ready feedback; remove unnecessary clicks and clutter.
16. **Deterministic State & Boundary Convergence:** Distinguish transient UI, cached observations and canonical state; fence stale/parallel interactions, guard external effects and never create phantom success.

**Conflict resolution:** True data-integrity and route/account boundaries override convenience; routine validations should nevertheless run automatically. If a hard rule conflicts with an accepted product behavior, **surface the contradiction for a specific client decision** instead of silently holding the entire feature forever.
